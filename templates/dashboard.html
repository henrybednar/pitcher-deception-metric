Deception+

Deception+ measures how far a pitcher's results land above or below what a model predicts from each pitch's speed, spin, movement and location, plus the batter, catcher, count and park.

How a score is built

  1. Predict

    A model rates every pitch from its speed, spin, movement, location, the batter, catcher, count and park.

    {{EX_N}} swings

  2. Compare

    Actual results minus the prediction, averaged over the pitcher's pitches.

    +{{EX_RAW}} points

  3. Shrink

    Small samples are pulled toward average, so luck does not look like skill.

    +{{EX_SHRUNK}} points

  4. Scale

    Counted in standard deviations above average, then set to 100 plus 10 per step.

    {{EX_Z}} steps, so {{EX_INDEX}}

  5. Blend

    {{N_COMPOSITE}} areas (whiff, chase, weak contact, timing, whiff miss distance) are averaged, steadier ones weighted more.

    {{TOP1_SCORE}}

Worked example: {{TOP1_NAME}}, {{TOP1_SEASON}}, whiff area. Points are percentage points of whiff rate per swing.

How to read a number

100 is average and 10 points is one step. Every score has a 95% interval on the leaderboard. Check it before ranking two close pitchers.

What it does not prove

It is whatever the model cannot explain, so calling it deception is a hypothesis. It tracks Stuff+ (r={{STUFF_R}}), and sequencing, tunneling, spin mirroring, tempo and arm slot each explain at most {{DRIVER_R2_MAX_PCT}} of it.

Does it just track Stuff+?

Partly. They correlate at r={{STUFF_R}} (95% interval {{STUFF_R_CI}}): better stuff tends to score higher, but Stuff+ explains only about {{STUFF_R2_PCT}} of the differences in Deception+.

The model accounts for each pitch's measured traits, but Stuff+ knows more than those measurements.

Stats key

r (correlation)
How closely two numbers move together: 0 is no link, 1 is a perfect match.Pearson r.
Reliability
Odd and even games scored separately, then compared. High means skill, low means luck.Split-half r, Spearman-Brown corrected.
AUC
How often the model rates a pitch that drew a whiff above one that did not. 50% is a coin flip.Area under the ROC curve, out of fold.
Calibration slope
Do predicted rates match real ones? At 1.0, a predicted 30% happens about 30% of the time.Actual on predicted rate, by decile.
R²
The share of differences a model explains. 0.23 means 23%.Cross-validated, grouped by pitcher.
Forecast gain (ΔR²)
Extra share of next season a piece explains once added. Timing's {{DELTA_TIMING}} is {{DELTA_TIMING_PCT}} points.Change in cross-validated R².
p
How often a gain this big would appear by luck if the piece added nothing. Under 0.01 passes here.Errors clustered on pitcher.
95% interval
The range likely to hold the true value. If it clears zero (a gain) or 100 (a score), the result is clear.Scores: wider of bootstrap and analytic.
Percentile
The share of pitchers a score beats.Among scored pitcher-seasons.

Before you quote a score

  • Volume matters: year-to-year r is {{YOY_QUAL_R}} when the pitcher qualified both seasons, {{YOY_R}} overall.
  • Relievers average {{RP_MEAN}} against {{SP_MEAN}} for starters even after adjusting for fatigue, so part of the gap may be role.
  • Left-handers average {{LHP_GAP}} points against right-handers (p={{LHP_P}}). The gap is real or leftover model error, and these data cannot say which.
  • Three seasons means no aging curves and only two season-to-season pairs.
