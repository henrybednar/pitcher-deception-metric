Deception+: pitcher results beyond what the pitch's measured traits predict

Deception+ scores how far a pitcher's actual whiff, chase, weak contact, swing timing, and whiff miss distance results land above or below what the pitch's measured traits predict. The prediction uses velocity, spin, movement, release, location, the batter, the catcher, the count, and the park. 100 is the qualified-pitcher average and 10 points is one standard deviation, the same scale as Stuff+.

How to read it

What the score is

The gap between what happened and what a model expected from the pitch's measured traits, the batter, the catcher, the count, and the park. All 5 gaps are combined, each weighted by how reliable it is.

What it does not show

Why a pitcher beat the results their pitch's measured traits predicted. The score is not independent of pitch quality (it correlates 0.386 with Stuff+), and sequencing, tunneling, spin mirroring, tempo, and arm slot were tested as explanations and none accounts for the gap (cross-validated R² -0.01 to 0.06 at the season level, -0.0003 to 0.0015 at the pitch level). Calling the gap deception is a hypothesis.

How good are the pitch models?

Deception+ is actual minus expected, so the expectation models carry the score. Each outcome has gradient-boosted models by pitch type (similar types share one where that helped), and every prediction is out of fold: folds are grouped by pitcher, so a pitcher's own pitches never help predict their own results. Whiff AUC runs 0.76 to 0.86 across pitch types and chase 0.82 to 0.89 (0.5 is chance). For the 5 members the calibration slopes run 1.00 to 1.09, where 1.0 is calibrated. Timing and whiff miss distance explain little of their outcomes (R² 0.07 to 0.23 and 0.10 to 0.41), so most of their variation stays unexplained, and reliability and the forecast test decide what enters the score. The methodology page has the AUC, log loss and calibration charts for every model.

Does it just track Stuff+?

Partly. Among 1,291 qualified pitcher-seasons with a Stuff+ figure, Deception+ correlates r=0.386 with Stuff+ (95% interval 0.32 to 0.45, resampling pitchers; by season, 0.312 in 2024, 0.454 in 2025, and 0.397 in 2026), and each member does too (whiff .304, chase .228, weak contact .231, timing .137, and whiff miss distance .353). The model controls for each pitch's own measured velocity, spin, movement, release, and location, but Stuff+ contains information beyond those measurements, so the score still tracks it. Stuff+ comes from FanGraphs, was entered by hand, and the model never uses it.

Does it hold up?

Reliability is a split-half correlation (odd against even games, Spearman-Brown corrected). The forecast columns test whether a pitcher's score in one season predicts their rate the next season after accounting for what that next season's own expectation already predicts, with every pair of consecutive seasons stacked. ΔR² comes from 5-fold cross-validation grouped by pitcher, and p from a test of the added term with standard errors clustered on pitcher. A p below 0.01 counts as a pass.

Pitchers with a reputation for deception

Eight pitchers scouts often call deceptive. Tarik Skubal's value comes from whiffs (whiff 138, ground ball 82, Deception+ 145). Tyler Rogers's comes from ground balls (whiff 67, ground ball 130), which Deception+ leaves out on purpose, so his Deception+ is 102. The signed timing readout, which is not part of the score, has hitters making late contact by 5.0 inches on average against Rogers, measured against each hitter's ideal contact depth.

Read it with care

  • The median 95% interval on Deception+ is about ±13 points, so only 288 of 1,291 qualified scores (22%) are clearly different from 100. Check the interval on the leaderboard before ranking two close pitchers.
  • Stability depends on how much a pitcher threw. Among pairs of consecutive seasons in which the pitcher qualified in both, the year-over-year correlation is 0.682. The headline 0.611 includes small samples.
  • Qualified relievers average 101.2 against 99.6 for starters, even with in-game fatigue and times through the order in the model, so part of the gap may be role. The leaderboard can filter by starter or reliever.
  • Left-handed pitchers average +1.4 points against right-handed ones (361 against 930 qualified pitcher-seasons, p=0.10). Handedness is a model input, so the gap is either real or a leftover miscalibration, and these data cannot tell which.
  • Three completed regular seasons (2024 to 2026), so there are no aging curves and only two year-over-year pairs.
